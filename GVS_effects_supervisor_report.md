# GVS effects analysis report

Source folders reviewed: `Claude_results` and `figures/GVS_effects`.

## Short conclusion

Across the saved analyses, the most consistent GVS effect was not a large behavioural change, but a small brain-network effect. Active GVS tended to reduce the task/weighted-network projection relative to sham, especially in the OFF-medication session, and edge-level coactivation/connectivity analyses found many FDR-significant active-minus-sham network changes. Behavioural RT effects were weak/trend-level, decoder/searchlight analyses did not classify GVS reliably, and most ROI-level mass-univariate tests did not survive correction.

## Data analysed

- Main GVS network analyses used 18 subjects, 35 sessions, 70 runs, 5463 trial-level ROI rows, and 38 left/right AAL-derived weighted ROIs.
- Behaviour analyses used 5359 finite behaviour trials from 18 subjects, 35 sessions, and 70 runs.
- Conditions were sham plus eight active GVS waveforms: pink noise, DC+1, DC-1, delta, theta, alpha, beta, and gamma. Most analyses compared active GVS with sham, separately for OFF and ON medication where possible.

## Methods and main results

| Analysis type | Method used | Main result |
|---|---|---|
| Behavioural RT and RT variability | Subject/session/run-level active-GVS minus sham paired tests, with waveform-specific FDR correction. | Any active GVS was slightly slower than sham at subject level: mean RT delta = +5.44 ms, 95% CI -0.93 to 11.81, p = 0.089. RT variability was not significant: p = 0.674. Waveform-specific effects were exploratory; GVS5/theta had the largest mean RT slowing (+13.83 ms, raw p = 0.0119, FDR q = 0.0949). |
| Convergent behavioural profile | Earlier waveform-specific RT summaries by medication. | Several raw behavioural effects appeared, but none survived FDR. Delta and beta in OFF, and delta/theta in ON, showed raw RT decreases, but corrected q values were > 0.15. |
| Weighted-network projection | Projected trial/beta signal onto the task-derived voxel/ROI weight map; tested each active waveform against sham. | OFF medication showed significant negative projection changes after FDR for DC+1 (mean = -0.119, p = 0.0120, q = 0.0481) and theta (mean = -0.0705, p = 0.00876, q = 0.0481). ON medication showed negative means but no FDR-significant waveform. |
| Collapsed any-GVS network projection | Collapsed all active GVS conditions into an active-vs-sham subject/session network score; sign-flip tests preserved pairing. | Trend-level negative network delta in OFF: mean = -0.0125, p = 0.0886. Pooled OFF+ON was p = 0.128; ON alone was p = 0.506. |
| Threshold sensitivity | Recomputed projected-network effects with p85, p90, and p95 voxel-weight thresholds. | Best result was p85 in OFF: mean = -0.0124, sign-flip p = 0.0450, but this did not survive FDR across thresholds (q = 0.170). This supports a small negative OFF-session network effect but should be treated as sensitivity evidence. |
| Block-level mixed-effects model | Mixed model on block-level network deltas with medication and random effects. | Fixed active-GVS network effect was not significant: intercept/mean delta p = 0.442; medication term p = 0.635. Random variance terms were present, indicating block/run heterogeneity. |
| ROI-level group tests | Paired group t-tests, Friedman omnibus tests, ROI x GVS mixed models, multivariate permutation tests, and any-GVS ROI summaries. | Most ROI x waveform effects did not survive correction. In the older combined summary, only two ROI-condition cells were marked significant by the LME analysis: OFF Thalamus_L GVS7 and ON Hippocampus_R GVS7; the other four analysis families did not confirm corrected significance. |
| Nested run/block ROI-burden sensitivity | Within subject/session/ROI models and burden heatmaps counting FDR-significant ROI cells across GVS conditions. | The nested mixed-model sensitivity found 12 FDR-significant cells in OFF and 12 in ON, spread across 4 OFF subjects and 6 ON subjects. However, the block-mean paired t-test found only 1 ON FDR cell and the run-block sign-flip permutation found 0, so this result is exploratory and sensitive to model choice/convergence. |
| Motor/reward ROI profiles | Focused ROI summaries for motor/sensorimotor, basal ganglia-thalamus, and reward/limbic ROIs. | Several raw effects were seen in motor and limbic ROIs, but no global-medication FDR-significant ROI waveform effects. This is useful descriptively, not as a strong corrected result. |
| Circuit-level aggregation | Aggregated active-minus-sham network effects within broad circuits: motor/sensorimotor, basal ganglia-thalamus, reward/limbic, cortical association, cerebellar. | No circuit survived FDR. The strongest trend was ON motor/sensorimotor: mean = -0.0305, sign-flip p = 0.0994, q = 0.615. |
| Connectivity/coactivation | ROI coactivation/connectivity changes during active GVS vs sham; included mean correlation metrics and edge-level sign-flip/FDR analyses. | The strongest global coactivation summary was ON mean correlation delta: +0.00619, sign-flip p = 0.0438, q = 0.0876. Edge-level analyses were much stronger: mutual-information connectivity found 156/703 FDR-significant edges, with 46 increased and 110 decreased; Spearman/Pearson found 194/703 FDR-significant edges. Many strongest edges involved amygdala, olfactory, thalamic, frontal, and sensorimotor regions. |
| Connectogram/network-density comparison | Compared significant edge density by network, hemisphere relation, anatomical group, and task-vs-main result sets. | Main result density was 156/703 edges = 22.2%, with more decreases (110) than increases (46). However, hemisphere and network-family enrichment did not survive BH correction. Task-vs-main density differences also did not survive q < 0.05. |
| Multivariate network perturbation | Permutation test on the norm of the 38-ROI group mean active-minus-sham delta vector. | Not significant: OFF p = 0.877, ON p = 0.948. This means the full ROI vector did not show a reliable group-level perturbation under this test. |
| Similarity-to-sham matrix analysis | Descriptive distance/similarity of active-GVS ROI-trial matrices relative to sham. | Active-GVS matrices were clearly non-identical to sham by the distance metric (sign-flip p = 0.0002), but this was treated as descriptive effect-size evidence rather than a primary null test. |
| Network variability and ROI dispersion | Tested whether active GVS changed network variance or ROI dispersion. | No FDR-significant effects. Best trend was ON ROI dispersion delta: mean = +0.0199, p = 0.128, q = 0.513. |
| Bayesian shrinkage approximation | Empirical-Bayes style shrinkage of ROI x GVS cells and posterior directional probability. | No cell reached 95% credible directional evidence. The top cell was ON GVS2 Supp_Motor_Area_R with posterior directional probability = 0.873, below the 0.95 threshold. |
| Predictive decoding | Cross-validated classifier for active GVS vs sham from ROI/network features. | Classification was at chance: all-session AUC = 0.477, OFF AUC = 0.460, ON AUC = 0.482. |
| Searchlight decoding | Voxelwise searchlight classification for stimulation/carrier targets with TFCE correction. | No FWE-significant voxels. Mean AUCs were approximately chance: stimulation OFF = 0.497, ON = 0.501; carrier OFF = 0.500, ON = 0.501. |
| Behaviour-linked network analysis | Correlated network GVS deltas with behavioural RT and RT variability summaries. | No FDR-significant brain-behaviour associations. The largest raw association was Spearman rho = 0.525 for network interaction vs mean-RT improvement, raw p = 0.0307, but FDR q = 0.935. |
| Projection-RT coupling | Tested active-vs-sham projection, RT, projection variability, and projection-RT coupling. | No significant subject-level effects: projection p = 0.127, RT p = 0.641, projection-RT coupling p = 0.893. |
| Alternative FC edge tests | Paired edge tests using mutual information, Pearson correlation, and partial correlation; also node strength summaries. | Several raw edge/node results appeared, but none survived edge/node FDR. Example: mutual-information paired edges had 12 raw p < 0.05 but minimum FDR p = 0.407; Pearson edges had minimum FDR p = 0.925; partial correlations had minimum FDR p = 0.532. |
| FC pattern PCA | PCA of functional-connectivity change patterns, followed by component tests. | No PCA component survived FDR. PC1 explained 12.3% variance and had p = 0.244 by Wilcoxon, p = 0.215 by t-test, and permutation p = 0.221. |
| Medication/FC structure analyses | ON-minus-OFF FC comparisons for intra-ROI vs between-ROI and within- vs between-hemisphere FC. | This was a medication-effect analysis rather than a direct GVS effect. The strongest result was increased intra-ROI voxel FC relative to between-ROI FC under medication: contrast mean = 0.01535, paired t p = 0.00369; within-vs-between hemisphere FC was not significant (p = 0.675). |

## Overall interpretation

The results suggest that GVS produced small but detectable changes in the task-weighted brain network and in ROI-to-ROI coactivation structure, especially in OFF medication and especially as decreases in projected network signal or connectivity. The behavioural response-time effect was small and only trend-level. ROI-specific and decoder/searchlight analyses were mostly negative after correction, so the strongest defensible statement is that GVS affects distributed network organization more than it produces a robust single-ROI or behavioural effect in this dataset.
